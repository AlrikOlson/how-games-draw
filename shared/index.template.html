How Games Draw

Twenty-two hands-on lessons on how a game turns triangles into the picture on your screen, one idea at a time. Every lesson walks you through a demo you drag and poke at, asks you to guess what will happen, then shows you. No graphics background needed: each idea starts in plain words, and the real math and code sit under the hood for when you want them.

Three things to know

The timing numbers in the lessons (milliseconds, memory, bandwidth) are rough estimates unless a source is named right next to them. Engines, games and papers are named only where the author was sure; otherwise the lesson just says "many engines".

The demos are flat, 2D versions of 3D techniques, built that way on purpose so you can see the moving parts: a strip of pixels instead of a full screen, an outline instead of a mesh, a side view instead of a camera cone. The math and code shown are the real 3D forms.

This course was built with AI. Alrik Olson directed it and checked the results; the lessons, demos and code were written with GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1. Every demo's steps were tested by a script before publishing, and the claims that were written from memory are listed in the project's notes rather than passed off as sourced.
